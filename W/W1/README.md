# W1 — Weapons as Systems

*Components, Infrastructure and Human Organisation*  
**Zawwar Sami** · W Series · **Independent preprint v1.0** · 14 October 2026 · 18 pages

A military capability is more than the performance of its visible equipment. This paper examines the relationships between equipment, supporting infrastructure, people and time, and asks which evidence is needed before a technical claim becomes a claim about sustained service.

A finite construction holds equipment, qualified-personnel and infrastructure availability at 75% each while their joint service ranges between 25% and 75%. I then apply a six-field claim profile to public F-35 readiness evidence, separate Patriot investigations, and the Berlin Airlift. The calculation is exact under its stated assumptions; these documentary examples are not a field validation of the proposed assessment format.

## A question to test

Separate availability percentages cannot identify joint service when their temporal dependence is unspecified. What is the least additional evidence needed for a defensible service claim—and when would an explicit capability-claim profile improve that judgment over existing assurance practices?
